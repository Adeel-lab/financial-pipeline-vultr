Ford Recalls F-Series Trucks for Speedometer and Tachometer Issue

Ford will replace the instrument cluster modules of 102,159 trucks at no charge to affected F-150, heavy-duty, and medium-duty truck customers
Published: 21 Oct 2026, 12:25 UTC
By:
Mircea Panait
  8 photos
Photo: Ford
Imagine pulling a trailer at what you think is 65 miles per hour. The speedo may read 65, then 62, and then stop moving altogether. That is precisely the problem affecting 102,159 trucks from the F-Series, which can be split between the F-150, the HD line, and the medium-duty family of workhorses.
   

All of these vehicles left their respective factories with analog gauges that may wander erratically or stick in place without warning. 26V653 is what the NHTSA calls this campaign, while 26C46 is Ford's number for it. Both 2025 and 2026 models are potentially affected by the described issue.

The heavier-duty pickups were produced between December 14, 2024, and April 17, 2026, a sole F-750 on November 21, 2025, and the F-150s between August 22, 2025, and May 12, 2026. Supplied by Visteon, the analog cluster modules are listed under a dizzying variety of part numbers in the recall documentation.

Ford claims that supplier process deficiencies are to blame for the reported concern, through the recall filing admits the Dearborn-based automaker still doesn't fully understand the deficiencies at play here. Nevertheless, the supplier allegedly fixed everything with tool maintenance back in January 2026.

As you're well aware, a needle that sticks or moves erratically means the driver loses the primary vehicle speed and engine speed readouts. The Ford Motor Company highlights that customers with a redundant digital speedo may notice a difference between the two displays, and that is the only warning identified by the filing published on the NHTSA's website.

Photo: Ford
Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 101 governs all things controls and displays, and a speedometer that doesn't work as intended can be considered a noncompliance problem. Although certain affected trucks meet the stated requirements, they may not meet the full intent of the rule, thus prompting this recall. The recall population, meanwhile, breaks down rather unevenly.

The heavy- and medium-duty lines are listed with a grand total of 91,589 trucks, starting with a single example of the F-750. The F-600 totals 2,827 units, followed by 12,260 of the F-550, 6,847 of the F-450, 26,354 of the F-350, and 43,300 of the F-250. Add 10,570 units of the F-150 to said figure, and you get 102,159 trucks.

Only 1 percent of the recall population is believed to actually contain the defect. While a small slice of a big number, 1 percent still means over 10,000 trucks at the very least. FoMoCo became aware of this headache back in May 2026, prompted by a growing number of reports alleging sticky or erratic tachs and speedos.

By September 16, the Dearborn-based manufacturer had counted 93 field reports and 18 warranty claims received between January 2025 and August 2026. The remedy comes in the guise of a replacement cluster module, validated for proper operation. Ford isn't ready to say when replacement parts will become widely available at dealers nationwide.

Interim customer letters will be mailed starting on October 26, 2026. Remedy notification letters are likely to follow in the first two weeks of December 2026.
