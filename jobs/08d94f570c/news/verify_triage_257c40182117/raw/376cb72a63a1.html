GM Plans Hybrid SUVs as It Broadens Strategy Beyond Battery-Electric Vehicles

By

FCW Team

October 29, 2026 at 8:39 AM EDT

Image: GM

  • General Motors plans to build hybrid versions of the Chevrolet Traverse, Buick Enclave and GMC Acadia at its Lansing Delta Township plant in Michigan, according to people familiar with the matter who spoke to Reuters, as the company broadens its powertrain strategy beyond battery-electric vehicles.
  • The Energy Department is restructuring a previously awarded $500 million Domestic Manufacturing Conversion Grant, with the funds now set to support future hybrid production at Lansing Delta Township.

General Motors plans to introduce hybrid versions of three of its stronger-selling three-row SUVs — the Chevrolet Traverse, Buick Enclave and GMC Acadia — at its Lansing Delta Township assembly plant in Michigan, according to people familiar with the matter who spoke to Reuters. GM confirmed in a statement that it plans to invest in the plant to prepare for future hybrid-electric vehicle production but did not provide details on timing or specific models.

CEO Mary Barra told analysts during GM’s earnings call last week that the company would bring hybrids to multiple segments. “We will have hybrids. They’ve already been added. It’s not going to be additional (capital expenditures) and engineering,” she said, adding that GM would provide more details closer to launch.

Market Share Pressure

The move comes as GM has lost ground in the US market to manufacturers with established hybrid lineups. GM’s US market share fell to 16.4% in the first nine months of 2026, from 17.1% a year earlier, according to the company, while hybrid-heavy Toyota and Honda gained ground. GM’s only current US hybrid is the Chevrolet Corvette E-Ray. US hybrid sales have grown as buyers seek lower fuel costs, and GM dealers have been pressing the company at regional meetings with sales executives to add hybrids to the lineup.

“We’ve been telling them every chance we get that we need hybrids,” one dealer told Reuters.

GM executives had previously said hybrids add too much cost and complexity and that the company preferred to invest in EVs.

Federal Funding Redirected

The Energy Department is restructuring a previously awarded $500 million Domestic Manufacturing Conversion Grant, with the funds now set to support future hybrid production at Lansing Delta Township. The grant was originally awarded under the Biden administration for EV-related manufacturing conversion. Energy Department Assistant Secretary Audrey Robertson was scheduled to tour the Lansing facility on Tuesday.
