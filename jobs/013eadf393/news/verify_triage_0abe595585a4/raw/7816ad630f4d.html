Ford 7.3L Godzilla V8 Under Federal Investigation Over Alleged Catastrophic Failures

One fleet owner reported nearly half his fleet needed a new engine in less than a year.

7.3L V8s Roar Could Be Snuffed Prematurely

The NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation has opened an investigation into camshaft and lifter failures in 2021-2022 Ford models with the big 7.3-liter V8. The list of vehicles under investigation includes the E-350 van, F59 chassis, and the F-750, though many other Ford models use a version of the same engine.

ODI has just opened the investigation after it received a petition in June requesting one on the trucks. Under various federal and state regulations, NHTSA must respond to petitions alleging vehicle safety issues.

This petition alleged that low oil pressure at idle causes "severe wear on lifter rollers and camshaft lobes," and leads to "catastrophic engine failure and sudden loss of motive power while driving at highway speeds." It also alleges that Ford was aware of the problem after it released a software patch that raised idle speed in an attempt to solve the issue but failed to notify owners.

A cam or lifter failure is highly destructive and most likely requires a complete engine replacement. We've reported on the teardown of a failed 7.3 that showed the consequences.

What Happens Next?

Right now, ODI is investigating to see if there is a defect worth pursuing. If it finds that, then there will be a lot more investigating to find out how widespread the problem might be, the root cause, and then if it could turn into a full recall.

The petition that led to ODI's initial investigation is not available on the safety agency's site. However, there are other complaints listed, including one from a fleet owner in North Carolina. The complaint was made in February 2024, and of that fleet's 11 Ford models with the engine, five failed. The earliest failure was at just 45,745 miles, the longest-lived at 103,177.

Ford's Godzilla 7.3-liter V8 is the engine it launched to replace the 6.8-liter V10 and 6.2-liter V8 in serious trucks. It launched in the Super Duty line for model year 2020 and was added to the E-Series and bare chassis the next year. Pickup truck versions of the engine made 430 horsepower and 475 pound-feet of torque in earlier years. Van engines made 300 or 325 hp and 425 or 450 lb-ft, and chassis engines made 335 hp and 468 lb-ft.

Ford also sells this engine as a performance crate engine called Megazilla. That one makes 615 hp and 640 lb-ft.

While the investigation only covers a few Ford models with the engine, there are still around 50,000 vehicles involved. The same pump was used across all 7.3-liter V8s, though Ford has replaced the part number at least six times, suggesting newer models may not suffer the same problem.

CarBuzz Insight – Why This Matters:

For now, there's nothing for most owners to do. The investigation has started, but there's no fix or guarantee of a recall. However, if you've had to repair or replace one of these engines, it's probably a good time to send your paperwork to the NHTSA. That's one of the ways ODI decides if a recall is warranted, and if there's a recall, you could get a refund for those costs.
