    Key Takeaways

    • Positive Sentiment: Q3 adjusted EBIT rose 3% to $2.7 billion, supported by strong truck and SUV mix, cost reductions, and record auto-adjacent revenue. Ford raised the low end of its 2026 adjusted EBIT guidance, now expecting $10.5 billion to $11 billion.
    • Positive Sentiment: Ford Pro and Ford Credit remained important earnings contributors, with Ford Pro generating $1.7 billion of EBIT and Ford Credit posting $803 million of EBT. Software services and parts contributed about 10% of quarterly revenue at accretive margins, while Ford Pro expects growth from Oakville capacity and commercial leasing.
    • Positive Sentiment: Ford Energy reported strong early demand for grid-scale battery storage, with executed contracts and late-stage negotiations covering more than 80% of expected 2028 capacity. The company remains on track to bring 20 gigawatt-hours of annual capacity online by the end of 2027, with roughly two-thirds of its planned $2 billion investment expected next year.
    • Neutral Sentiment: Ford’s affordable Fathom EV remains on track for a late-2027 launch with a starting price of $28,350, and 90% of validation testing is complete. However, supply-base readiness remains a key remaining risk, and Model e is expected to incur higher expenses in 2027 before profitability improves more substantially in 2028-2029.
    • Negative Sentiment: Ford expects 2027 profitability to be roughly in line with 2026 despite investments in new vehicles, the Universal EV platform, and Ford Energy. Management anticipates approximately $2 billion of headwinds from commodities, memory, and logistics, while European operations continue to face pressure from Chinese competition, EV mandates, and industry overcapacity.
