    Ford calls the Escape's exit a 'breather' as report points to a 2029 electric comeback

    The Cool Down
    Jennifer Green
    Sun, November 1, 2026 at 5:45 AM UTC
    Add to Preferred Sources
    Sun, November 1, 2026 at 5:45 AM UTC

    Ford's Escape SUV may be getting a second chance as an electric vehicle after the automaker ended production of the gas-powered model.

    A new report points to 2029 as a possible return date for the SUV, whose gas-powered run lasted 25 years. Ford has left the door open, saying the "SUV may be taking a breather for now," after ending production.

    Here's what to know

    Louisville Assembly Plant is the proposed production site for the electric Escape, with manufacturing expected to begin in 2029, Electrek reported. The crossover would use Ford's Universal Electric Vehicle platform, per Ford Authority.

    Advertisement
    Advertisement

    With the Fathom electric pickup scheduled to launch in 2027, the Escape could be next in line to use the platform.

    The electric model is expected to have dimensions similar to the outgoing Escape, which Electrek lists as having a 106.7-inch wheelbase, a length of 180.1 inches, a width of 74.1 inches without mirrors, and a height of 66.1 inches.

    Hyundai's Tucson and Toyota's RAV4 would therefore remain compact-SUV peers for an Escape of that size.

    More background

    Shoppers visiting the Escape's page on Ford's website are currently directed to other Ford vehicles. The Edge's page, by contrast, describes that model's run as over.

    Advertisement
    Advertisement

    The reported Escape revival would give Louisville another EV to build after the Fathom pickup. Electrek says the factory's conversion for Fathom manufacturing coincided with the end of gasoline Escape production.

    For Escape shoppers considering an EV as their next car, potential benefits include lower fuel costs and reduced routine maintenance. However, the report provides no Escape EV price, driving range, or charging specifications.

    What's being done?

    Ford's more immediate effort to offer an affordable electric vehicle centers on the Fathom.

    A $1,595 delivery fee brings the starting cost to $29,945, keeping it below $30,000. Preorders are scheduled to open in early 2027, with the standard-range version starting at $28,350.

    Advertisement

    The pickup's standard equipment will include Apple CarPlay, Android Auto, and bidirectional charging. Electrek also says the vehicle will be BlueCruise-capable for hands-free driving and will debut Apple Maps integration in its navigation system.

    During Ford's third-quarter earnings call, CEO Jim Farley described the pickup as "a breakthrough product."
